    Enhancement Catas

    Dossier for ML and AI research · Independent engineering practice · Research edition / 14 October 2026

    Expertise is not yet a business.

    An advanced engineer does not need another catalogue of AI tools. The missing system connects an expensive, observable client problem to a bounded intervention, a credible evaluation, a safe deployment and a commercial arrangement that supports continued ownership. This dossier develops that system from ten selected videos and a separately gathered technical evidence corpus.

    The core recommendation is to begin with a narrow paid diagnostic, prove a measurable result in a restricted pilot, and expand only when data access, operational ownership and economics justify doing so. Sell a reduction in a named business problem; do not sell “AGI transformation,” an unqualified accuracy promise or an architecture diagram without a measurement plan.

    What this edition contains

    An executive summary, ten four-page video dossiers, chapter maps, consulting frameworks, production engineering schematics, ML validation guidance, security boundaries, financial examples, twelve repository inspection cards, a ninety-day operating plan and a closing section on how I apply the method in client work. The interactive edition offers search, page navigation, reading progress, a pricing-floor calculator and click-to-load YouTube embeds. The PDF edition uses the same content.

    Read this evidence boundary first

    YouTube metadata and published chapter descriptions were retrieved for all ten videos. Caption endpoints returned empty responses. These pages are therefore not transcript-complete summaries. Chapters are mapped faithfully where available; deeper implementation guidance is explicitly editorial synthesis supported by separate sources. No invented quotations, unseen speaker statements or guaranteed income outcomes are included.

    Executive summary

    The whole dossier in one page: what to sell, how to deliver it, and where the evidence stops.

    The problem this dossier solves

    Technical skill is not yet a business. What turns it into one is a repeatable system: find an expensive, observable client problem; scope a bounded intervention; prove it with a credible evaluation; release it safely; and price it so the work can be owned and supported over time.

    Eight conclusions

    • Sell diagnosis before implementation. Start with a narrow, paid diagnostic that produces a baseline, a data-readiness assessment and a go/no-go decision.
    • Sell a named business problem, not a technology. “Reduce time spent reconciling support policies while preserving citations” qualifies a buyer; “RAG engineer” does not. Do not sell “AGI transformation” or unqualified accuracy promises.
    • Treat evaluation as a deliverable. Agree in-scope inputs, material mistakes, sampling and the person who accepts the result before the pilot starts. Compare against a deterministic baseline.
    • Use the simplest adequate architecture. Rules, conventional ML, retrieval, workflows and agents each have a place; added complexity must answer a measured failure.
    • Earn autonomy in stages. Offline evaluation, then shadow use, then supervised production, then narrowly authorized action. Authorization belongs to deterministic controls, never to the model.
    • Price from a floor, then stress-test. Compute the hourly floor from compensation, overhead, reserve and realistic utilization, and test fixed fees against low, central and adverse effort.
    • Manage capacity and concentration. An anchor client helps cash flow but raises dependency risk. Budget time for sales, admin, evaluation, support and reusable assets.
    • Use repositories as evidence of interfaces, not of safety. Pin a commit, read the license and tests, and reproduce a narrow example before proposing a tool to a client.

    The decision-ready executive brief

    Build the smallest commercially useful system that you can evaluate and safely operate.

    The commercial thesis

    Start by selecting one buyer, one recurring workflow and one business consequence. A “RAG engineer” label describes a supply-side capability; “reduce time spent reconciling support policies while preserving source citations” describes a buyer problem. The second is easier to qualify because the client can identify volume, current effort, error costs and an accountable process owner. A discovery conversation should test whether the consequence matters enough to fund a change, not merely whether the client is interested in AI.

    The delivery thesis

    Treat evaluation as a purchased deliverable. Before a pilot, agree which inputs are in scope, which mistakes matter, how evidence will be sampled and who accepts the result. Implement a deterministic baseline and compare the proposed AI system with it. Release in stages: offline evaluation, shadow use, supervised production and only then narrowly authorized action. Keep rollback and manual work paths available.

    The operating thesis

    Your economic constraint is not maximum coding speed. It is total capacity after sales, administration, evaluation, support and downtime. An anchor engagement may improve cash-flow predictability, but one dominant client also increases concentration and dependency risk. Price expected work and uncertainty separately; reserve time for maintaining reusable assets and building independent lead sources. Keep client-specific data out of those assets.

    The research thesis

    Use public repositories to understand architecture, interfaces, licenses and failure surfaces—not as proof that a deployment is safe. Pin a commit, inspect tests and configuration, verify the license files and reproduce a narrow example before proposing it to a client. Source-visible software may restrict commercial hosting or embedding. AGI remains a research horizon in this dossier: sell verifiable capabilities available in a defined deployment, not speculative general intelligence.

    For each chapter, make one decision and create one artifact. Examples include a list of excluded inputs, a labelled error sample, a signed acceptance rubric or a repository license inventory.     07

    01 · Launch the consultancy

    Engagement architecture

    EDITORIAL SYNTHESIS · Sell phases, not an unbounded promise.

    Separate uncertainty from delivery

    A diagnostic establishes whether the problem is feasible and valuable; a pilot measures performance on a limited workflow; implementation integrates and hardens the chosen design; operations supports a live service. These are different purchase decisions. A client should be able to stop after the diagnostic and still own a useful report, baseline and decision. Bundling every stage into a single fixed price before data access moves unknown risk onto the engineer and encourages rushed acceptance.

    Make phase gates explicit

    For a diagnostic, specify access needed, interviews, data sample, decision memo and exclusions. For a pilot, define supported inputs, reference baseline, evaluation set and acceptance criteria. For implementation, define identity integration, environments, monitoring, rollback and training. For support, state response windows and excluded enhancements. Distinguish a response-time commitment from a resolution guarantee and avoid promising around-the-clock support as a solo operator.

    Control client expectations

    Show the difference between a persuasive demo and a production system. A demo may work on selected examples, use manual preparation or omit access controls. Document those gaps before the client forms a procurement expectation. Explain that model behavior is probabilistic but service ownership is not: the contract still needs an accountable owner, incident process and bounded scope. Unknowns become paid investigation items rather than silently absorbed work.

    Artifact and failure test

    Create a stage-gate sheet with deliverable, evidence, approver, payment event and stop condition. Ask: can the client accept this stage without relying on my verbal explanation? If not, the deliverable is underspecified. Test the pause scenario: when data is delayed, who pays for reserved capacity and what happens to dates? Put the agreed answer in the commercial documents before beginning.

    01 · Launch the consultancy

    Outbound with business evidence

    EDITORIAL SYNTHESIS · The strongest outreach starts with a verifiable workflow hypothesis.

    Choose a trigger you can observe

    Useful signals include a public hiring push for a repetitive function, an announced product expansion, a documented support backlog or a platform migration. A signal suggests a possible problem; it does not prove the organization has that problem. Phrase the message as a hypothesis and invite correction. Avoid claiming internal insight from public data or using scraped personal information merely because it is technically accessible.

    Build a minimum credible message

    Name the buyer’s workflow, the likely friction, a narrowly relevant proof asset and a low-pressure next step. For example: “Your published knowledge base spans several product versions. I built a citation-checking support prototype; would a short discussion about version-specific retrieval be useful?” The proof asset should show scope, representative failures and measurement—not just a polished chatbot. Keep outreach truthful and comply with consent, privacy and anti-spam requirements for the recipient’s jurisdiction.

    Measure conversion at each stage

    Track researched accounts, delivered messages, relevant replies, qualified meetings, diagnostics proposed and diagnostics won. Open rates are unreliable and can create privacy burdens; business-stage movement is more actionable. If replies are weak, revisit relevance. If meetings do not qualify, revisit targeting. If qualified opportunities do not close, inspect urgency, authority, evidence and commercial clarity before increasing message volume.

    Do not automate a broken approach

    Test manually on a small set of appropriate accounts before building tooling. Never represent an automated message as personal research if it was not. Maintain suppression lists, an opt-out process and contact provenance. A sales automation agent should draft rather than send until policy and quality are proven. The engineering skill is valuable when it strengthens evidence and consistency, not when it produces more irrelevant outreach.

    Estimation without false certainty

    EDITORIAL SYNTHESIS · An estimate is a model of work, dependencies and uncertainty—not a precise promise.

    Decompose the engagement

    Estimate discovery, data preparation, evaluation, implementation, integration, security review, deployment, documentation and project management separately. Include client dependencies: access approvals, domain-expert reviews, procurement and acceptance. Distinguish effort from elapsed time. A task taking two engineering days may sit behind a two-week security approval, and a fixed calendar deadline may require reserved capacity even when the client is blocked.

    Use ranges and assumptions

    Provide low, central and adverse scenarios for uncertain work. Identify which assumption makes each scenario plausible and what evidence would narrow it. A data spike may determine whether source documents have stable identifiers or whether OCR repair dominates the project. Do not present a contingency percentage as scientifically derived when it is an editorial planning buffer. For high uncertainty, sell a bounded investigation before quoting the implementation.

    Link change control to evidence

    Define a change as a new input class, integration, acceptance threshold, deployment region or requirement not in the agreed baseline. Record the request, impact, options and client decision. Some discoveries are contractor errors and should not automatically become chargeable changes; allocate responsibility fairly. Keep an assumption log so both parties can see when an agreed premise has changed.

    Check economic exposure

    Model the proposed price against expected total hours and an adverse case, including support and rework. If the downside would threaten the practice, reduce scope, change the commercial model or decline. Milestones should reflect meaningful delivered value, not arbitrary fractions of time. Maintain a reserve for uncertainty, and distinguish cash collected from revenue recognition and after-tax owner income.

    02 · Data, dollars, deliverables

    Sell diagnosis before implementation

    EDITORIAL SYNTHESIS · A client request is a starting hypothesis, not the final specification.

    Ask what must change

    When a buyer asks for an agent, ask what outcome the agent is expected to create, why the current process fails and who experiences the consequence. The desired intervention might be search, better data quality, an interface change or staff training rather than autonomous execution. A technically advanced consultant creates value by finding the smallest adequate intervention, including when that intervention is not AI.

    Observe work, not just opinions

    Request a walkthrough using a realistic case. Capture decisions, source systems, exceptions, handoffs, time spent and failure consequences.     State the workflow, affected population, current baseline, target improvement, constraints and owner. Separate a business outcome such as reduced handling time from a technical measure such as retrieval hit rate. Technical success can coexist with no business value if users do not adopt the system or reviews take as long as the original task. The diagnostic should connect both measurement levels.

    Provide options: improve the existing process, implement a deterministic automation, add bounded AI assistance or defer. Compare evidence, costs, uncertainties and risks. A useful diagnostic can recommend not proceeding; this is a feature of independent advice, not a sales failure. The resulting memo becomes the basis of a bounded next phase rather than an obligation to purchase your preferred architecture.

    EDITORIAL SYNTHESIS · A driver tree makes the value hypothesis explicit and falsifiable.

    Qualification and opportunity gates

    A sales funnel should protect delivery quality as well as generate work.

    Architecture selection: rules, ML, RAG or agents

    Complexity earns its place through measured need.

    Start with the task

    Use rules for stable deterministic logic. Use conventional ML for prediction or classification when representative labelled data and an evaluation design exist.     Compare with a baseline

    A keyword search or rule-based extraction can outperform a more expensive model for narrow inputs. Architecture decisions should be tied to observed failure classes.

    Bound autonomy

    Define allowed tools, data scope, maximum steps, spending limits, timeouts and actions requiring approval. Separate planning from executing irreversible changes. A system with dynamic reasoning can still have deterministic authorization. If an autonomous approach cannot be safely recovered, reduce its role to recommending or drafting.

    Record the decision

    Create an architecture decision record with context, options, evidence, chosen design, constraints and revisit triggers. A multi-agent system adds coordination, observability and failure surfaces. The simplest adequate design is not unsophisticated; it preserves capacity for evaluation and operational quality.

    Use identical representative cases to compare options. Record task success, critical-error count, review effort, p50/p95 latency, estimated cost per completed task and integration burden.     RAG schematic and retrieval diagnosis

    A grounded answer depends on retrieval, authority and output validation.

    Ingestion path

    Parse documents, retain source metadata, split with structure awareness, attach access labels and index. Evaluate chunk boundaries against actual question types. Version the corpus and transformations. A vector store is not a source-of-truth permission system; enforce authorization at retrieval and response time.

    Query path

    Authenticate the user, resolve access scope, retrieve candidates, combine lexical and semantic signals where useful, rerank and build bounded context. Handle empty or contradictory evidence explicitly. Do not allow instructions inside retrieved documents to override system policy or authorize tools.

    Measure separate failures

    Track whether relevant evidence was retrieved, whether the answer used it correctly and whether citations support claims. Retrieval metrics alone do not establish answer quality. Evaluate by document type, query category, source freshness and permission boundary. Include questions the system should decline or escalate.

    Improve from observed errors

    A stale answer may require version filters; a missing answer may require parsing repair or different chunking; an unsupported statement may require stronger response constraints and grading. Do not assume embeddings are always the bottleneck. Keep a manual path and disclose the limitations of the corpus.

    Failure-to-intervention matrix

    Missing source text suggests parsing or ingestion repair. Correct document but wrong passage suggests chunking, query formulation or ranking investigation. Superseded policy suggests version filters and authority rules. Relevant evidence with unsupported output suggests response constraints, citation validation and generation grading. Unauthorized content suggests a permissions defect and requires containment, not merely a prompt change. Contradictory authoritative sources require an explicit escalation rule. Test one intervention at a time on held-out representative cases where feasible; avoid redesigning the entire pipeline before identifying which stage failed.

    Evaluation contract and error-analysis loop

    Evaluate the business task, not a vague impression of intelligence.

    Define success

    Write task-specific criteria with domain experts: what makes an answer useful, correct and acceptable? Distinguish critical failures from cosmetic issues. Use binary gates where decisions require clarity, with qualitative critiques to preserve nuance. A single average score can hide dangerous slices.

    Build a representative set

    Include real cases when permitted, realistic synthetic cases, known failures and boundary cases. Record provenance and label quality. Synthetic examples can bootstrap testing but are not proof of production representativeness. Hold back cases used for final comparison and watch for leakage through prompt tuning.

    Inspect traces

    Review failures manually, group root causes and choose the next experiment based on severity and frequency. A useful viewer exposes inputs, sources, tools, output and reviewer critique with privacy controls. Count experiments and evidence, not just shipped features. Keep rubric changes versioned.

    Make evaluation continuous

    Run regression tests when prompts, models, tools, retrieval or policies change. Add newly observed failures to a controlled dataset. Calibrate automated judges against expert labels and monitor criteria drift. Report uncertainty and slice results rather than turning a small sample into a universal reliability claim.

    Dataset and sampling protocol

    Define the unit of evaluation: a query, case, document or complete workflow. Record task slice, expected behavior, severity, provenance and reviewer. Include ordinary inputs and known edge classes without pretending their proportions match production unless measured. Deduplicate related examples and prevent the same underlying document or entity from leaking across tuning and evaluation when that matters. Report sample composition alongside results. A small set can detect regressions and guide experiments, but cannot establish a rare-event failure rate with high confidence.

    LLM judges: calibration and failure modes

    A judge model is an instrument that needs validation.

    Specify a rubric

    Describe the exact criterion and provide examples of pass, fail and edge cases. Avoid vague quality scores that different reviewers interpret differently.     Protect the grading boundary

    Treat content being evaluated as untrusted. It may contain instructions attempting to influence the judge. Keep grading instructions separate, constrain outputs and validate the result.     Maintain trust

    Version rubrics, example sets and judge configuration. Recalibrate after model or policy changes and audit a sample of production grades. Use human review for high-consequence decisions. Automated grading can scale a well-defined process; it cannot repair an unclear success criterion.

    Agent workflows and safe action gates

    The model may propose actions; authorization belongs to deterministic controls.

    Choose a pattern deliberately

    Prompt chaining fits sequential tasks with intermediate checks. Routing fits distinct task categories. Parallelization fits independent subtasks or multiple assessments. Orchestrator-worker designs fit decomposition that cannot be fully specified in advance. Evaluator-optimizer loops fit tasks with clear feedback.     Control execution

    Validate tool arguments against typed schemas, enforce permissions outside the model and limit network and filesystem access. Use idempotency keys for external writes, explicit approval for consequential actions and maximum step budgets. Separate tool-result text from instructions. Never let a retrieved page grant additional authority.

    Recover state safely

    Checkpoint meaningful state, record tool calls and distinguish planned from executed actions. A retry can duplicate an external side effect if idempotency is absent. Define cancellation, rollback or compensation and the manual escalation path. Timeouts and errors should be visible, not hidden in a long agent loop.

    Evaluate the trajectory

    Check task outcome, authorized behavior, side effects, cost and recovery—not only the final answer. Replay in a stable test environment using mocks for risky tools. Include interruption and malformed-output tests. An agent that completes a task through unauthorized access is a failure, not a success.

    Side-effect state machine

    Use proposed, validated, awaiting_approval, approved, executing, executed and failed states with authorized transitions. Bind approval to immutable action arguments and an expiry, not merely a conversation message. Record an external operation identifier and idempotency key before retrying. After a timeout, check whether the target completed the action rather than issuing a duplicate write automatically. Define compensation where rollback is unavailable. A model may generate a plan, but it must not directly grant itself approval or increase its own tool permissions.

    MCP and tool-boundary security

    Interoperability does not automatically create trust.

    Define the trust boundary

    Conventional ML: validation and leakage

    Sound predictive modeling remains central to advanced engineering.

    Split according to the real task

    Use temporal splits for forecasting and grouped splits when related entities would otherwise appear in both train and test.     Use decision-relevant metrics

    Assess the cost of false positives and false negatives, calibration, class imbalance and performance by relevant groups or time slices. Compare with simple baselines.     Connect deployment to monitoring

    Version data, features, configuration and artifacts. Monitor input changes, prediction distributions and outcome performance when labels arrive. Drift is a signal to investigate, not automatic proof that retraining will help. Plan retraining, rollback and ownership around the actual workflow.

    Temporal feature-availability test

    For every feature, ask whether its value existed when the prediction would have been made. A payment status updated after default, a support resolution written after escalation or a revised forecast published after the target date can leak the answer. For time-dependent tasks, build features from an as-of snapshot and evaluate on later periods. For entity-dependent tasks, keep related entities separated where the use case requires generalization. Document whether the model predicts new entities, future periods or repeated observations; these are different validation questions.

    Fine-tuning versus retrieval and prompting

    Choose the intervention that matches the failure class.

    Diagnose the gap

    Retrieval can supply current or private evidence; prompting can clarify task behavior and examples; fine-tuning can target learned behavior or task adaptation when appropriate data exists.     Test cheaper changes first

    Assess dataset rights and quality

    Confirm permission to use examples for training, retention and provider processing. Remove inappropriate personal information and preserve task representativeness. Keep training, validation and test boundaries. Synthetic data can be useful but may reproduce model errors and narrow diversity; review it against real requirements.

    Evaluate lifecycle cost

    Compare task quality, latency, inference cost and operating complexity. Version the training data and model artifacts. Plan how factual updates and behavior regressions will be handled. A customized model still requires output validation, permissions and monitoring. Do not equate a fine-tuned model with AGI or guaranteed correctness.

    Experiment register for adaptation

    Record failure class, candidate intervention, dataset rights, baseline, evaluation split, target measure, budget and stop rule. Compare prompting, retrieval repair and training on the same task definition where practical. Track data-preparation and maintenance effort as well as inference cost. If factual freshness is the issue, assess how updates will be supplied after training. If behavior is the issue, verify improvement on independent examples and critical failure slices. Do not choose training because it sounds more advanced; choose it when evidence supports the lifecycle tradeoff.

    The ninety-day launch playbook

    A sequence of artifacts and decision gates, not an income promise.

    Days 1–20: validate the entry offer

    Inventory disclosure-safe experience, choose one buyer/workflow hypothesis and prepare one proof asset. Hold a small number of appropriate buyer conversations.     Use targeted, lawful outreach and existing relationships. Propose a bounded paid diagnostic when there is fit. Produce a workflow map, baseline, data-readiness memo, options and scope recommendation. Gate: evidence of feasible value and a client owner willing to support evaluation. The schedule is illustrative; procurement may take longer.

    Days 46–70: execute a measured pilot

    Create representative tests, a deterministic baseline and the minimum adequate system. Review failures with domain experts. Report slice performance, operating cost, review burden and remaining risk. Gate: agreed acceptance evidence, clear integration path and appropriate governance. A no-go result is an acceptable outcome.

    Days 71–90: operationalize and learn

    Complete staged deployment, training, recovery rehearsal and handover where justified. Agree support separately. Obtain permission for a case study and ask for relevant referrals after recognized value. Review realized economics, pipeline and concentration. Choose whether to repeat, narrow, partner or pause based on evidence—not an arbitrary growth target.

    Weekly operating review

    Review pipeline stages, booked capacity, invoice status, delivery blockers, evaluation evidence, support obligations and unresolved decisions. Pick one commercial experiment and one delivery improvement for the next week. Compare actual paid utilization with the financial model. Track whether client work creates permissioned proof and repeatable methods. If the pipeline is weak, diagnose targeting and offer fit before buying outreach automation. If delivery is overloaded, reduce commitments before accepting another contract. This review is a proposed practice, not an automated schedule or future promise.

    Reusable asset kit and decision log

    Create artifacts that reduce repeated ambiguity.

    Commercial artifacts

    Maintain an offer sheet, discovery agenda, qualification form, proposal structure, scope baseline, assumption log and change record. Keep legal templates professionally reviewed and versioned. Reuse structure, not client-confidential content. Record who approved each commercial decision.

    Ownership artifacts

    Track accounts, repository rights, licenses, dependencies, model providers, data retention and support responsibilities. Identify who can revoke access and restore service. Do not put secrets in spreadsheets or exported dossiers. Use a secret manager approved for the engagement.

    Decision log format

    Each record contains date, owner, question, options, evidence, assumptions, chosen action and review trigger. Preserve disagreement and confidence. When the result differs from expectation, compare against the original assumptions rather than rewriting history. This creates a reusable learning system across engagements without leaking client data.

    Decision record example

    Question: should the support pilot move from draft-only to ticket updates? Evidence: task-quality evaluation, authorization tests, idempotency tests and operator rehearsal. Options: remain draft-only, permit supervised updates or defer. Assumptions: approved reviewer available and target API supports safe duplicate prevention. Owner: client workflow sponsor. Review trigger: new input class, material failure or changed tool permission. Record the chosen action and rationale. Do not approve greater autonomy based solely on a few successful demonstrations; the authority boundary changes the risk profile.

    Original schemas and executable boundaries

    Illustrative artifacts to adapt and test—not production-ready integrations.

    Evaluation-case schema

    Every test should name its workflow, input, expected behavior, severity, provenance and review owner. Include cases requiring abstention. Do not store confidential payloads in the public dossier. The illustrative schema below is original and framework-neutral; it is not copied from a repository.

    Tool policy schema

    A tool needs an argument contract, permission scope, timeout, maximum attempts and side-effect classification. A policy engine must enforce these outside the model. An approval field is not sufficient unless execution verifies the approval and binds it to the exact proposed action. Replay protection and idempotency require implementation against the target service.

    State-machine interpretation

    Transitions from draft to approved to executed must be explicit. A failure after execution but before recording creates ambiguity; use a durable transaction record or the target system’s idempotency mechanism. A model should not be able to update the approval state by writing persuasive text. These are design requirements to test, not claims that a snippet solves distributed consistency.

    Version and rights

    Attach dataset, rubric, model and code versions to results. Keep third-party notices when actual code is reused. No network service, model endpoint or repository dependency is executed by these examples. Use an isolated test environment and qualified review before adapting them to consequential client workflows.

    Glossary and distinctions

    Words that need operational definitions before they appear in a proposal.

    AI, ML and AGI

    AI is an umbrella term for systems performing tasks associated with intelligence. ML learns patterns from data. Generative AI produces content conditioned on inputs. AGI has no operational definition adopted by this dossier and is not sold as a deliverable. Specify the actual task, environment and evidence instead of relying on broad labels.

    RAG, workflow and agent

    RAG retrieves information to inform generation; it does not guarantee factual correctness. A workflow follows predefined control paths. An agent dynamically selects steps or tools within a designed environment. All still need authorization, validation, evaluation and recovery. “Agentic” is not a safety or quality certification.

    Evaluation, benchmark and acceptance

    Evaluation measures behavior for a defined task and dataset. A benchmark is a particular comparison setup, not proof of fit for all clients. Acceptance is the commercial decision that agreed deliverables satisfy agreed criteria. Business outcomes can lag acceptance and depend on adoption or conditions outside contractor control.

    Accuracy, reliability and safety

    Accuracy must identify the prediction or task, dataset and metric. Reliability concerns consistent service behavior over relevant conditions. Safety concerns unacceptable harm and its controls. A high average score can coexist with unsafe failures. Describe important slices and severity, not merely an aggregate percentage.

    Open source and source available

    Public code visibility does not establish unrestricted rights. Review license terms, notices, dependencies and the intended hosting or distribution model. A permissive license is not a warranty of correctness or security. Source-available terms can allow some uses while restricting others.

    Diagnostic, pilot, production and support

    A diagnostic produces evidence for a decision. A pilot tests a bounded intervention. Production integrates operating controls and ownership. Support provides defined ongoing obligations. A prototype demonstration should never silently become an unlimited production promise.

    Work with me

    How I apply this for clients

    AI agents and LLM automation for small businesses, built on .NET/C# and full-stack engineering, and delivered in three stages.

    Who this is for

    Small and growing businesses with a recurring, expensive workflow: an overloaded inbox, documents re-typed by hand, knowledge buried in policies and PDFs, or leads and tickets that need triage. You don’t need an AI strategy to start. You need one workflow whose cost you can name and someone who can say what a good result looks like.

    Stage 1 · Paid diagnostic

    A short, fixed-scope engagement. I map the workflow, measure today’s baseline, check data access and quality, and write up the options with their risks. You get a workflow map, a data-readiness memo, a draft evaluation rubric and a clear go/no-go recommendation. A “no-go” is a valid and useful outcome. It costs far less than building the wrong system.

    Stage 2 · Measured pilot

    A bounded build against agreed acceptance criteria. I assemble a representative test set, build a deterministic baseline, and then build the minimum adequate system. Depending on the task, that might be rules, a retrieval (RAG) assistant with citations, typed document extraction, or a tool-using agent behind approval gates. I report results by slice, cost per completed task and reviewer workload, not a single headline score. Consequential actions stay in draft until a person approves them.

    Stage 3 · Production and handover

    Staged release from shadow mode to supervised use, and to narrowly authorized actions only where the evidence supports it. Production work includes logging and traces, health checks, retries with backoff, idempotent external writes, rollback paths, a runbook and a handover rehearsal. Ongoing support is agreed separately, with defined obligations.

    What I build with

    • .NET/C#: ASP.NET Core APIs and LLM gateways with provider abstraction, rate limits, backoff and server-side key handling.
    • Full-stack: React/TypeScript front ends, Python (FastAPI) and Node.js services, deployed on Linux with Docker, nginx and Cloudflare.
    • LLM and agent tooling: OpenAI-compatible APIs, Gemini and local models via Ollama; structured outputs, retrieval, evaluation harnesses and trace review.

    The same pattern runs through my own systems. JobNet drafts every outbound message for human approval before anything is sent. Mercatus Assist is an ASP.NET Core 8 gateway that routes Gemini through an OpenAI-compatible interface, with backoff and keys kept server-side. Both are described on my portfolio.

    What I won’t do

    Promise guaranteed accuracy or “AGI”; automate irreversible actions without an approval step; scrape behind logins, paywalls or anti-bot controls; or use data you are not authorized to share. Legal and privacy questions, including POPIA where South African personal data is involved, are reviewed with your qualified advisers.
